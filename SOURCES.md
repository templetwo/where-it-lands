# Sources & fact-check ledger

The essay stakes its credibility on a line in its own appendix: *"a piece about
honesty should hold itself to it."* This ledger holds it to that. It lists the
citations as printed in the PDF and records a verification status for each.

Verification levels:
- **Verified** — checked against the primary record / source this pass.
- **Consistent** — matches the standard historical record and my knowledge; not independently re-pulled this pass.
- **Interpretive** — a defensible reading the essay already hedges; flagged so a future editor sees it.

## The load-bearing claim — VERIFIED

**Ramanujan → mock theta functions chain.** The essay's most precise, most
falsifiable citation, and the one to verify first if this ever goes public,
because the whole appendix rides on it.

- Claim: framework from **Sander Zwegers (2002)**; **Kathrin Bringmann and Ken Ono (2006)**; **Michael Griffin, Ken Ono and Larry Rolen** proved Ramanujan's examples satisfy his own definition, **published PNAS 2013**, "roughly ninety years" after his 1920 letter.
- **Verified (June 2026):** Griffin, Ono & Rolen, *"Ramanujan's mock theta functions,"* **PNAS 2013, vol. 110, no. 15, pp. 5765–5768** — the first rigorous proof that all of Ramanujan's mock theta functions satisfy his original definition, building on Zwegers' 2002 thesis and Bringmann–Ono. 1920 → 2013 = 93 years ≈ "roughly ninety." The claim holds exactly.
- PNAS: https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1300345110 · PMC: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3625272/

## Two interpretive notes (the essay already hedges both — flagged for any editor)

- **Hilma af Klint "double-helix-like" / "first abstract canvases."** The 1915
  work contains a *spiral* form; "double-helix-like" is an interpretive
  description. "First abstract in Europe" is contested on priority grounds, but
  the narrower claim the essay actually leans on — *before Kandinsky* (whose
  first abstracts date ~1910–11, vs. af Klint's Temple series from 1906) — is
  defensible. The appendix already phrases it carefully.
- **Blake "called mad by Wordsworth."** Commonly cited, source-debated; the
  appendix correctly softens it to "reportedly" and reframes him as "more
  neglected than persecuted." Honest as written.
